Pillar 2: Learn By Writing

写 (Xiě) • Stroke Rules & Tian Zi Ge Pad

Chinese character stroke order follows systemic structural rules. Mastering stroke sequence builds muscle memory and speeds up character recall!

Core Character Construction Rules

1. Top to Bottom (从上到下) e.g., 三 (sān - three), 言 (yán)
2. Left to Right (从左到右) e.g., 明 (míng - bright), 你 (nǐ)
3. Horizontal then Vertical e.g., 十 (shí - ten), 木 (mù)
4. Frame Before Inside e.g., 国 (guó - country)

Pillar 3: Learn By Listening

听 (Tīng) • The 4 Mandarin Tones

Mandarin pitch contours define meaning! Listen to how the 4 tones modify pitch, then challenge yourself in Tone Master.

1st Tone (55)

mā (妈)

High & Level Pitch

Sustained high voice pitch.

2nd Tone (35)

má (麻)

Rising Pitch

Rises like asking "Huh?"

3rd Tone (214)

mǎ (马)

Dipping & Rising

Dips low then curves upward.

4th Tone (51)

mà (骂)

Sharp Falling Pitch

Sharp drop like stating "No!".

Pillar 4: Learn By Reading & Culture

读 (Dú) • High School Radicals & Component Logic

Chinese characters are composed of meaningful radicals. Over 80% of characters combine a meaning radical with a phonetic clue!

亻

Person Radical (rén)

Derived from 人. Indicates human relationships, actions, or roles.

Examples: 你 (You) • 他 (He) • 们 (Plural)
氵

Three-Water Radical (shuǐ)

Derived from 水. Found in words associated with liquids, rivers, and washing.

Examples: 河 (River) • 洗 (Wash) • 海 (Ocean)
讠

Speech Radical (yán)

Derived from 言. Associated with words, speaking, languages, and studying.

Examples: 话 (Speech) • 语 (Language) • 说 (Speak)
