Pillar 2: Learn By Writing

写 (Xiě) • Stroke Rules & Tian Zi Ge Pad

Chinese characters are constructed following universal stroke order rules. Following these rules makes writing faster and more beautiful!

Golden Rules of Character Writing

1. Top to Bottom (从上到下) e.g., 三 (sān - three)
2. Left to Right (从左到右) e.g., 川 (chuān - river)
3. Horizontal then Vertical e.g., 十 (shí - ten)
4. Outside then Inside e.g., 月 (yuè - moon)

Pillar 3: Learn By Listening

听 (Tīng) • The 4 Mandarin Tones

Mandarin is a tonal language! Changing pitch changes the word meaning completely (e.g. mā = mother vs. mǎ = horse).

1st Tone (55)

mā (妈)

High & Level Pitch

Like saying "Aah~" at the doctor!

2nd Tone (35)

má (麻)

Rising Pitch

Like asking "What?!" in surprise.

3rd Tone (214)

mǎ (马)

Dipping & Rising

Like saying "Well..." slowly.

4th Tone (51)

mà (骂)

Sharp Falling Pitch

Like shouting "Hey!" firmly.

Pillar 4: Learn By Reading & Culture

读 (Dú) • Pinyin, Radicals & Culture

How do Chinese characters work? They aren't random lines—they are made of radical clues!

女

Female Radical (nǚ)

When you see 女 in a character, it often relates to family or people!

Examples: 妈 (mother), 姐 (older sister), 妹 (younger sister)
氵

Three Water Drops (sāndiǎnshuǐ)

3 drops on the left mean the character relates to water, liquids, or body of water!

Examples: 河 (river), 海 (ocean), 洗 (to wash)
木

Wood / Tree Radical (mù)

Characters with 木 usually refer to trees, forests, or objects made of wood!

Examples: 林 (grove), 森 (forest), 椅 (chair)
